Spec addendum v1.2
Decisions that close the gaps in Version 1.0. Values marked proposed need confirmation with your developer or lawyer.
1. Identity and shared devices

A device belongs to a post (Gate 2), not a person. Each guard has an employee number and a 4 to 6 digit PIN.

  • PIN required to log in, and again to clock in or out. Five wrong attempts lock the account until a supervisor resets it.
  • At shift change the outgoing guard clocks out and logs out. Nothing is ever recorded under the previous user.
  • A selfie at Duty On and at Duty From is mandatory (see section 11). Optional per-site setting, off by default: a one-time site check at Duty On (not tracking).
  • A supervisor can clock someone in on their behalf, but only with a reason, and it is audited.
2. Offline-first behaviour
  • Every action (clock in, task, report) is saved to a local outbox with a unique ID and device timestamp, so retries never create duplicates.
  • The outbox syncs automatically when signal returns. Text goes first, photos after, compressed.
  • The phone shows how many items are queued. Try it: tap the signal indicator on the phone.
  • Events up to 72 hours old are accepted and flagged "late-synced" for the supervisor.
  • Calls use cellular voice, so they work without data.
  • Conflicts: the server wins on shifts and task definitions. The device wins on events it created.
3. Trusted time
  • All official times are server time. The device clock is stored alongside for comparison.
  • When offline, attendance uses the device clock plus elapsed time since the last server sync. The server reconciles on upload.
  • Drift over 2 minutes is flagged. The MDM enforces automatic network time so guards cannot change the clock.
  • Grace period (proposed): clocking in up to 5 minutes after start is ON TIME.
4. Scoring rules v1 (all editable per company)
  • Score = 80 + points from the last 30 days, kept between 0 and 120. Daily cap of plus or minus 5 to stop gaming.
  • Bands: Above Par 90 or more. On Par 70 to 89. Needs Attention below 70.
  • Default points: on time +1, task done +1, training +1, report closed +1, outstanding +2, late −1, missed task −1, missed shift −2.
  • A task marked "Could not complete" with a reason carries no penalty until a supervisor reviews it.
  • Supervisors can award up to +2. Larger awards and every reversal need a manager. The events table is append-only: a reversal adds an offsetting event and never deletes.
5. Fairness and labour law
  • Points inform a conversation. They never trigger discipline, pay deductions or dismissal automatically.
  • Every negative event shows its evidence and has a Query button. Employee has 7 days to query and a supervisor answers within 3 working days.
  • No covert monitoring and no continuous location tracking.
  • I am not a lawyer. Before the pilot, ask an employment lawyer to review the scoring rules, the employee notice and whether worker or union consultation is needed.
6. POPIA and data handling
  • Employees sign a notice listing exactly what On Par records. Employees can see all of their own data.
  • Host in South Africa (AWS Cape Town or Azure South Africa North). Encrypt in transit and at rest. Separate each company's data with row-level security.
  • Photos should show work items, not people, wherever possible.
  • Proposed retention, confirm with legal: photos 12 months, attendance, tasks and performance events 3 years, audit log 5 years, training records while employed plus 3 years.
  • TSF registers an Information Officer and signs an operator agreement with the developer and host. Keep a breach-response procedure.
7. Roles and permissions
  • Employee: own shift, tasks, reports, score and evidence.
  • Site supervisor: assigned sites. Creates tasks, assigns and follows up reports, awards up to +2.
  • Company manager: whole company. Closes reports, approves overrides and reversals, edits scoring rules.
  • System administrator: setup, devices, approved contacts, users. Client or estate manager: read-only summary of their site.
8. Patrol types, rules and overdue alerts
  • A site defines patrol types. Example: A is an internal patrol, B is an external perimeter patrol, C is a guard room check-in that is just one QR scan. Each type has its own checkpoints and its own three rules: patrols per shift, minimum gap, maximum duration.
  • A guard chooses the type on the device. The app shows what is due and when the next one opens. Only one patrol can be active at a time. A single-scan type completes with its one scan.
  • Checkpoints within a patrol can be scanned in any order. Route order is not enforced.
  • Each patrol is proven by a QR scan with GPS lock. A scan counts only if the phone has a GPS fix with accuracy of 25 m or better and is within the checkpoint radius (30 m, proposed). A photographed code scanned elsewhere is rejected and logged. The MDM blocks mock-location apps and developer options. Repeat scans within 2 minutes are ignored.
  • Patrols per shift: the shift is split into equal windows, one patrol per window.
  • Patrol points: each site and shift pattern has one patrol points allocation, for example 6 at one site and 10 at another. It is shared equally across all required patrols, so every completed patrol earns its full share and completing all of them earns the whole allocation. A missed patrol simply earns nothing. A penalty for missed patrols can be added later (default 0). Fractions are kept and shown to 2 decimals. Weighting some patrol types more than others is possible later.
  • Minimum gap: the next patrol of that type cannot start until the gap has passed since the last one finished. The system warns when gap plus maximum duration do not fit in a window.
  • Maximum duration: the clock starts at the first scan. If the patrol is not finished in time, an alert goes to the supervisor. If nobody acknowledges within 10 minutes it escalates to the control room. The alert clears when the patrol is completed or the supervisor confirms the guard is safe.
  • Limits of the alert: the server can only run the timer once it receives the first scan. With no signal, the phone shows the countdown and sounds a local alarm, and the server learns of it on sync. This is a timeout alert, not a replacement for a panic button or man-down sensor, which stay on the future list.
  • Patrol point instructions: each patrol point can carry a special instruction shown when the guard arrives, a photo (off, optional or required) and a note (off, optional or required). Managers create patrol points and edit these settings.
  • Checkpoint checks: any checkpoint can also carry one or more checks. Types: a number reading with a unit and a limit (for example generator fuel level in litres, flag below 50), OK or Problem, or a photo. The guard scans the QR, GPS locks, then completes the checks. The checkpoint only counts once the checks are saved. A reading outside its limit, or Problem, raises an Amber report automatically into the close-out workflow. Readings are stored as data so trends can be reported later.
  • A guard who cannot finish a multi-checkpoint patrol gives a reason. It is marked partial with no penalty until a supervisor reviews it.
  • Offline: checkpoints are cached on the device. Scans are stored with device time and validated after sync. Location is captured only when a scan happens.
  • Settings are per site and shift pattern, so day and night can differ. New data: Patrol type, Checkpoint (belongs to a type), Patrol rules, Patrol instance, Scan and Alert.
  • Your current QR product: import its codes as checkpoints if it can export them. Later: NFC tags, geofence-only points, optional route order, live trail, control-room map.
9. Reports, from report to sign-off
  • Anyone can report in any category. Every report is routed automatically to the higher level: the site supervisor, plus the site manager for Red priority. Routing is configurable per site.
  • The higher level assigns it to a person from the people directory (staff or contractor, with name, role, phone).
  • Stages: Reported, Assigned, Actioned, Attendance checked, Job inspected, Closed. Every stage records who did it, their role, a note and the time. History is append-only.
  • Actioned: the assignee (for example a plumber) does the repair. In the MVP the supervisor records this for contractors. Later the assignee gets app access and submits their own job report, with photos, straight to the higher level.
  • Attendance checked: the higher level confirms the assignee actually attended. Later this can be backed by the contractor's own time and location stamp.
  • Job inspected: the security officer on site is sent an inspection task, checks the work and records Job done or Not fixed. Not fixed sends the report back to Assigned with the reason.
  • Guard follow-up: the guard who reported can follow up on his own report at any time once it is assigned, without waiting for the higher level. He records what he found (not started yet, work in progress, repair done and OK, or not fixed) with an optional note and photo. It is added to the report history and flagged for the higher level. "Repair done, all OK" after attendance has been checked completes Job inspected. "Not fixed" after the work was actioned sends the report back to Assigned. Other security officers on site can follow up too, and repeat follow-ups are allowed.
  • Closed: the manager signs off. The reporting guard receives a positive performance event. Guards see the current stage and who has it.
10. Assigned tasks and recurrence
  • A task has an assignee (a person or a post), a date, a time, and an evidence rule such as a required photo.
  • Recurrence: one-off, daily, weekly or monthly (same day each month), with every-N-hours possible later. Example: check all fire extinguishers on the 19th of every month.
  • Production behaviour: the server generates each occurrence on schedule, whether or not the previous one was completed. An occurrence not done by its deadline becomes overdue and then missed. The prototype only creates the next one when the current one is completed, which is a simplification.
  • All dates and times use Africa/Johannesburg server time. Every occurrence keeps its own history, evidence and exception reason.
11. Duty On and Duty From declarations
  • Arrival is Duty On and departure is Duty From. Logging Duty On opens a separate declaration screen. The guard cannot use the rest of the app until it is completed. Logging Duty From opens the departure declaration in the same way.
  • Duty On declarations, all three required: "I am fit and free of injury and ready to commence and complete my shift". "I have read the OB and understand the tasks for the day". "I have taken receipt of all equipment handed over from the previous shift, all in good order".
  • Duty From declaration: "I am fit and free of injury and departing from duty, I have handed over all assigned equipment and handed over any information required by the incoming shift".
  • A selfie is mandatory at both Duty On and Duty From. A Comments tab lets the guard record anything, for example a broken piece of equipment. He can also raise the comment as an equipment report, which enters the normal close-out workflow.
  • Each record stores the wording shown (with a version), server time, device, employee, selfie, comment and which statements were accepted. Records cannot be edited or deleted. If a declaration is left incomplete, attendance shows "declaration pending" for the supervisor.
  • Injury reports link back to that guard's Duty On declaration, so management can see what he declared at the start of the shift.
  • There is no automatic face matching in the MVP (it is on the excluded list). A supervisor can view the selfie next to the registration photos. Automated matching could come later.
  • Have an employment lawyer review the wording and how it interacts with injury-on-duty claims. I am not a lawyer. A declaration is evidence, not proof.
12. Employee registration photos
  • Registration requires two photos: a close-up of the face, and the full body clothed in uniform. Registration cannot be completed without both.
  • Photos and selfies are personal information under POPIA, and can become special personal information if used to identify someone by their face. Get legal advice, tell employees exactly what is collected and why, restrict who can view them, encrypt them, and set a retention period (proposed: while employed plus a period agreed with legal).
13. Issued kit and re-orders
  • Employee registration has a panel for uniform and equipment issued, with sizes (shirt, trousers, jacket, boots or shoes, cap and so on) and asset numbers for equipment such as a radio. The list of standard items is set per company. Allocating an item creates or updates the record with an issue date.
  • In the guard's Report function there is a category called Re-order. The guard chooses Personal or Site.
  • Personal: he picks from the items issued to him. The system fills in his size or asset number from his profile, so an order for a new shirt is automatically in his shirt size. The comment gives the reason, for example damaged while chasing a suspect.
  • Site: free text, for example toilet paper, 2 packs. No fixed list.
  • Stages: Requested, Ordered, Assigned, Delivered, Received. Each records who, their role, a note and the time. Assigned means assigned to a person to deliver it (my assumption, please confirm). The guard confirms Received on his device, and for a personal item the issue date updates.
  • Later: approval limits, stock levels, supplier orders and cost tracking. Whether the company recovers the cost of a replacement from the employee is a labour-law question. Ask a lawyer before building any automatic deductions.
14. Enrolling a security officer
  • For now, enrolment is done by a management person on the management web dashboard (the Setup tab). Later, a management person can enrol and assign on the app itself. That needs a management login on the device: the role is decided at login, managers get a Manage section, and management functions are hidden on post devices unless a manager signs in with stronger credentials.
  • Personal tab: full name, SA ID number (13 digits, with a check-digit test), cell number, next of kin and their number, PSIRA number, PSIRA grade (A to E), PSIRA expiry date, and assigned site.
  • Qualifications tab: firearm competency, first aid, fire fighting and others, each with an expiry date and a certificate upload. Expiry dates feed the Expiring and Expired training figures on the dashboard.
  • Photos tab: four photos are required: face close-up, full body in uniform, ID document and PSIRA card.
  • Uniform and kit tab: the issued items with sizes and asset numbers (section 13).
  • Checks: the officer's grade is compared with the site's minimum grade, and an armed site needs a valid firearm competency. A failed check shows a warning. A manager override with a reason is proposed but not built.
  • Verify PSIRA registration against PSIRA's official records by hand for now. I have not assumed there is an integration.
  • The full ID number is stored securely and shown masked (last four digits). ID copies and photos are sensitive personal information, so restrict access, encrypt them, and check the rules with a POPIA specialist.
15. Creating a site and what it requires
  • A site has a name, address or area, client, a minimum PSIRA grade and an armed or unarmed flag.
  • Shifts: any number, each with a name, start time, end time and guards required.
  • Equipment per shift, as quantities: radios, torches, handheld devices, firearms and vehicles. The list of equipment types is configurable.
  • Approved contacts per site: supervisor, site manager and control room. These are the numbers the guard can call.
  • Patrol types, patrol points and patrol rules are set up after the site exists (section 8).
  • Coverage: the site shows officers assigned against the minimum needed for one day. Real rosters need relief guards, so proper rostering is a later feature.
  • Firearms are heavily regulated. Before building anything beyond a quantity, get advice from a firearms compliance specialist. Expect a register with serial numbers, sign-out and sign-in at shift change, and competency checks.
16. Employee portal, queries and notices (Phase 2)
  • Two doors: the shared post device for operational work on site, and a personal portal on the employee's own phone or any browser for personal matters. Personal and HR content is never shown on the post device, only a generic "you have a personal message" line.
  • The portal is a web application with a personal login (employee number, PIN and an SMS code are proposed). Links arrive as an app notification or SMS. A native personal app can come later. Keep paper and hand delivery for guards without smartphones or data.
  • Queries: one workflow for Pay, Hours worked, Leave, Uniform and kit, Personal details, Grievance and Other. Stages: Submitted, Received, Assigned, Responded, Closed. The employee confirms resolved or reopens it. Every stage records who, role, note and time. Queries go to HR or payroll, never to the site supervisor, and are confidential. Pay and hours queries can attach the employee's attendance record.
  • Notices: issued only by authorised HR or management, from templates a labour lawyer has approved. Types: verbal warning record, written warning, final written warning, notice of disciplinary hearing (with date, time and venue), hearing outcome and general message.
  • Delivery tracking: Sent, Delivered, Opened and Acknowledged, each with a time. Acknowledging receipt does not mean admitting anything. If a notice is not acknowledged within a set period (60 minutes in the demo, a longer configurable period in real use), HR is prompted to arrange hand delivery, and the signed copy is recorded. An app notification alone may not be valid notice, so ask a lawyer.
  • Warnings are never created automatically from scores. A manager may cite performance events as evidence.
  • Not built yet: investigation, hearing record and appeal stages, warning expiry, employee responses to notices, payslips and payroll integration. Check the private security sector bargaining council's rules before finalising the process.
  • Personal, pay and disciplinary data is more sensitive than operational data. Keep it in separate tables with its own roles and audit trail, and ask a POPIA specialist about retention.
17. Franchise model
  • Hierarchy: franchisor (TSF), then franchisee (each a separate company tenant), then site, then employee. Each franchisee's data is separated from the others.
  • TSF sees aggregated figures across franchisees (compliance, patrol adherence, incidents closed, training) but not personal HR data unless agreed in writing.
  • TSF publishes standard templates: patrol types, checklists, declaration wording, scoring defaults and notice templates. Franchisees adopt them and may customise within limits.
  • Branding: the TSF logo plus the franchisee's own where wanted. Onboarding a franchisee creates the company, an administrator and its sites.
  • No public app store is needed. Franchisee phones are set up through the MDM and the portal is a website. Charging (per guard, per device or a flat fee) belongs in the franchise agreement.
  • The franchisee is normally the employer and responsible party under POPIA. TSF's role and access need written agreements. Get legal advice before rollout.
18. Gamification (private, later)
  • Visible only after the guard's own login, never on a shared screen, a dashboard or to anyone else. It celebrates, it never ranks people against each other by default.
  • Levels (proposed names): Recruit, Officer, Senior Officer, Ace Officer, On Par Elite, based on a running count of positive events. This is separate from the 0 to 120 score, which stays the operational number management sees.
  • Streaks: consecutive on-time arrivals and consecutive completed patrols, shown on the guard's own performance screen.
  • Badges: earned for a pattern of good work, for example completing every patrol in a shift, or closing a report within the same shift. A supervisor could also hand out a small number of discretionary badges.
  • Celebration: a one-time private pop-up when a streak or level milestone is reached, for example three on-time arrivals in a row. It only ever congratulates, never criticises. Each milestone celebrates once.
  • A leaderboard is optional and, if built, should be opt-in or scoped to a team rather than naming individuals publicly, since a public ranking can demoralise as easily as it motivates.
  • Keep the existing safeguards: every point still shows why it was earned, and nothing here changes the rule that scores never trigger discipline automatically. Watch for guards optimising the game instead of the job, for example rushing a patrol to protect a streak; the patrol rules (minimum gap, GPS lock) already limit this.
19. Warning and inquiry document templates (Phase 2)
  • Template ladder: Verbal warning record, Written warning, Severe written warning, Final written warning, End of line memorandum, Notice to appear for disciplinary inquiry, Hearing outcome, General message. Each auto-fills from the employee record (name, employee number, site, date) and counts prior warnings of that kind on file.
  • Notice to appear for disciplinary inquiry is a compound document: the charge, hearing date/time/venue, the employee's rights (informed of the charge in a language and terms understood, reasonable time to prepare, representation by a fellow employee or shop steward, no interpreter since the inquiry is in English, the right to state a case and cross-examine witnesses, a written outcome with reasons and a right to appeal), a witness list HR builds item by item, and a representative field. HR can add or remove witnesses and the document text updates.
  • Every template is a drafting aid, not a legal document. A labour lawyer must approve the actual wording, the warning ladder, validity periods and the disciplinary process itself before real use. The private security sector's bargaining council rules may set requirements beyond this.
  • Suggested actions: the system can notice a pattern, for example three late arrivals with no written warning yet, and show HR a banner naming the pattern. Confirming the suggestion only prefills the notice form. It never issues, sends or auto-generates a warning. A person always decides. This preserves the existing rule that scores and event counts never trigger discipline automatically.
  • Other useful triggers to define later with HR: missed shifts, repeated task exceptions, patrol non-compliance, and a failed inspection after a follow-up. Each should surface as a suggestion only, with the same human-in-the-loop rule.
  • All issued notices keep the existing delivery tracking (Sent, Delivered, Opened, Acknowledged) and hand-delivery fallback from section 16. Records are immutable once sent.
20. Electronic Occurrence Book, EOB (next step, not yet built for real)
  • The EOB is an automatic, time-ordered activity log, assembled from everything already recorded elsewhere: Duty On and Duty From declarations, attendance, task completions and exceptions, every patrol scan and checkpoint check, every stage of every report, every stage of every re-order, notice delivery events and query stages. Nothing is entered twice; the EOB reads from the same records the rest of the app already keeps.
  • It does not replace the site's official Occurrence Book, which is a legal document and continues exactly as it does today. On Par's EOB is a supplementary, searchable view for site and management visibility, and should say so clearly on screen until a decision is made otherwise.
  • Layout: time, category, entry and who recorded it, as a single wide table for a full day at a site. On a tablet or desktop this is naturally wide; on a phone it is naturally better read in landscape, so the page should support rotation rather than assume portrait only.
  • Later: filter by date range, guard, site or category; export to PDF for handover or an audit; free-text entries a guard or supervisor can add directly for anything not already captured by another module; search.
  • Retention should match or exceed whatever the real Occurrence Book's retention requirement is, since this becomes a second record of the same day.
21. Firearm allocation and return, armed sites
  • Trigger: a site is marked armed (section 15). For an employee assigned to an armed site, Duty On gains a third tab, Firearm, alongside Declaration and Comments, and it must be completed before Duty On can be confirmed. The same site's Duty From gains a Firearm return tab, required before Duty From can be confirmed.
  • Allocation (Duty On): the firearm assigned to that post (make, calibre, serial number, drawn from a site armoury register); rounds of ammunition issued; holster issued and fits; bullet-proof vest issued and worn; a condition check (functions correctly on a dry check only, never a live-fire test; no visible damage to the firearm or holster; safety catch engages and disengages correctly); a photograph of the firearm and its serial number; and a safe-handling declaration the guard ticks line by line (trained in safe handling, keeps it holstered with the safety engaged unless there is immediate lawful need, never points it at a person unless lawfully justified, reports any loss, theft, discharge or malfunction immediately, and accepts personal accountability for the firearm and ammunition while it is in their possession).
  • Return (Duty From): rounds returned; condition on return (OK, or damaged or malfunctioning); whether there was a loss, theft, discharge or malfunction during the shift, with a note if so; a photograph; and a final declaration confirming the firearm, holster and all issued ammunition have been returned and any incident reported.
  • Automatic escalation: if the rounds returned do not match the rounds issued, the condition is marked damaged, or an incident is reported, a Red priority report is raised automatically to the site supervisor and site manager, titled as a firearm return discrepancy, and enters the normal report workflow. This is the one place in On Par where an automatic Red report is justified, because it is a safety-critical physical asset, not a performance judgement of the employee.
  • Every allocation and return is immutable once confirmed, appears in the guard's Duty declarations on the management dashboard, and appears in the Electronic Occurrence Book (section 20) as its own Firearm entry.
  • This does not replace a proper firearm register, competency records (section 14 already requires firearm competency for armed sites), or the legal requirements around firearm custody, transport and storage under the Firearms Control Act. Get advice from a firearms compliance specialist before real use, as flagged in section 15. A full armoury module (multiple firearms per site, assignment by serial number to a specific post, maintenance and service history) is a later build; the prototype demonstrates the workflow with a single fixed demo firearm.
22. Shift patterns and rostering (revised)
  • A shift pattern is a named, repeating cycle of Day, Night and Off, for example "3 day / 3 night / 3 off" or "5 day / 2 off", each with a two-digit ID (01, 02, ...) assigned automatically. Patterns are created and edited on the Roster tab and are reusable across sites.
  • Every site shift is tagged Day or Night (set when the shift is created or edited, section 15). A pattern's D and N symbols map to whichever of the site's shifts carries that tag, so a pattern is portable between sites even if their shifts are named or timed differently.
  • Allocation: site, shift pattern, employee, start date and position. Position is where in the pattern the employee begins, so the first two people on a "3 day / 3 night / 3 off" pattern start at position 1 (day one of the cycle) and the next two at position 4 (the first night day of the cycle), exactly as described. From the start date, every future day is computed automatically as (position plus days elapsed) modulo the pattern length.
  • Validity rule: a Night shift immediately followed by a Day shift is invalid and blocked, because a typical night shift ends at 06:00 and a day shift begins at 06:00, giving no rest at all. A Day shift immediately followed by a Night shift is valid: the day shift ends at 18:00 and the next occurrence of night only begins at 18:00 the following day, a full 24-hour gap. This is what makes the classic "3 day, 3 night" pattern work. The check applies cyclically, including the wrap from the last day of the pattern back to the first.
  • Simplification to correct later: the validity rule assumes the common 06:00 to 18:00 and 18:00 to 06:00 convention. A site with different shift times should really validate against its own actual start and end times rather than the symbol alone; the prototype does not yet do this.
  • The roster view shows one week at a time (previous and next week navigation) for a chosen site: a requirement row per shift type, drawn from the site's own guards-required numbers; one row per allocated employee, computed automatically from their pattern and position; an actual row per shift type, counting who is really on that day; and a red or green indicator when the actual count is short of, meets, or exceeds the requirement.
  • Manual override: clicking any single employee's cell on any single day changes just that day for just that person, without touching their underlying pattern or affecting any other day. This is the "change one shift for one person for a specific reason" capability, and it takes precedence over the computed value from then on for that date.
  • The employee portal's My roster view reads the same computed schedule, read-only, for the same displayed week.
  • The attendance register report (below) is built from this roster: the scheduled shift and hours per day, compared against the guard's real Duty On and Duty From times where they exist.
  • Not yet built: swap requests between employees, leave and relief cover, a conflict check for one person allocated to two overlapping patterns, and multi-week or indefinite scheduling beyond a single displayed week at a time (the underlying model already supports any future date; only the display is limited to one week).
On Par Roster: guided new-site setup
  • Branded "On Par Roster", a three-step guided flow launched from the Roster tab, for standing up a brand new site end to end: 1) Site, its shifts (each tagged Day or Night), equipment and approved contacts. 2) Staff and allocate: tick which existing employees join this site, and set each one's shift pattern and starting position. 3) Review, showing day-one coverage against the site's own requirement, then a single "Allocate, and create the site" action that creates the site, moves each ticked employee onto it, and writes their roster allocation in one step.
  • After Go, the wizard closes and the normal roster view opens already on the new site, ready for the small manual adjustments described above (section 22).
  • Site names must be unique; creating a site with a name already in use is blocked with a message rather than silently overwriting the existing site.
  • Editing a site afterwards uses the same inline edit already available from the site list (section 30); the Roster tab also has a direct "Edit this site" shortcut to it.
Clash prevention, device visibility, and scope (fixes and audit)
  • One site at a time, enforced, not just displayed. All allocation, whether through the wizard or the plain Allocate card, now goes through one function that removes any existing allocation for that person before adding the new one, so a person cannot end up rostered to two sites in the data itself. A red clash banner also appears at the top of the Roster tab if this invariant is ever violated regardless of cause, and the wizard warns before you tick someone in if they are currently rostered elsewhere, since reassigning them is valid but should never be silent.
  • Not a payroll or accounting system. Confirmed as an explicit exclusion, not just an omission: no pay rates, no salary calculation, no deductions, no leave-pay calculation. The attendance register (section 32) is evidence for payroll, not payroll itself, and should stay that way; if this is ever revisited, it is a distinct, much larger build with its own tax and compliance requirements, not an extension of this feature.
  • Visible to the guard in both places. The guard's own kiosk device now shows his real scheduled shift for today (site, shift name, times) computed from the roster, or "Off today", or "Not yet rostered" if nothing has been allocated, plus a short "Coming up" list of the next few days. This is in addition to, not instead of, the fuller My Roster view on his personal portal.
  • Grade and firearm competency cross-checked at allocation time, not only at enrolment. The wizard now warns, per person, if their PSIRA grade is below the site's minimum, or if the site is armed and they have no firearm competency on file. This does not block allocation, since a manager may have a reason to proceed and record an exception, but it makes the mismatch visible rather than silent.
  • Deeper issues worth deciding on before this goes further, not yet built:
    • The pattern validity rule (section 31) assumes every site uses the common 06:00/18:00 split. A site with different shift times should validate against its own actual start and end times, not the D/N symbol alone.
    • An employee's "home site" and their "currently rostered site" are presently the same single field. Real operations sometimes need someone temporarily on another site's roster (relief cover) without changing who they are normally employed under. These should probably become two separate concepts.
    • No weekly-hours check. A demanding pattern could put someone over a reasonable working-hours limit with no warning. This is a labour-law question (ordinary and overtime hours under the BCEA), not one to decide in this brief; flag it to the owner and a lawyer, and consider a configurable weekly-hours warning once the rule is confirmed.
    • Site requirements are flat across every day of the week. Many real contracts need different guard counts on weekends or public holidays; the model does not yet support that.
    • No leave, absence, or relief-guard workflow, and no swap requests between employees, both already flagged in section 31.
    • The roster display still shows one week at a time; the underlying computation already supports any date, so a longer view is a display change, not a data-model change.
  • Payroll month. Each site has its own payroll month start day, set when the site is created (default the 26th, so the month runs 26th to 25th, giving a few days to collate before paying on the 1st) and editable afterwards. The Reports tab's attendance register (section 32) is built around this period, not a calendar week, with previous and next period navigation. The weekly roster grid itself stays a calendar week for day-to-day operational use; only the payroll-facing report uses the payroll month.
23. Reports module (first report: attendance register)
  • A dedicated Reports tab, intended to grow into a small library. The first report is an attendance register, by employee, the record used to pay someone.
  • It combines the roster (scheduled shift and hours, from section 22) with the employee's actual Duty On and Duty From times where they exist, and shows hours worked and a status per day (on time, absent, rest day, and so on).
  • It is explicitly not a payslip: no pay rate, deduction or leave calculation happens here. The prototype only holds one live day of real attendance data, so only today's row shows genuine Duty On and Duty From times; every other day in the displayed week shows the schedule with no record, which is a limitation of the single-day demo, not the design.
  • Likely next reports: patrol compliance, an incident and report summary, training and PSIRA compliance, and a re-order and kit summary, each ideally exportable to PDF or CSV for handover, audit or a client.
